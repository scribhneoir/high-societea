<blockquote><p>For what do we live, but to make sport for our neighbours, and laugh at them in our turn?</p></blockquote>
<figcaption>— Mr. Bennet, <cite>Pride and Prejudice</cite>, ch. 57</figcaption>
</figure>

The gala takes place in rounds. Every third round, a dance will occur. Per turn,
a player may take one of the following actions. Round turns are played in
clockwise order, beginning to the left of the Host.

# On the Art of Conversation

Being engaged in a conversation takes up a character's engagement portion of the
turn. If a character wishes to speak with someone who is already engaged in
conversation, they may interrupt or tear them away from the initial
conversation. Doing so may have consequences.

# On Dancing

## Asking Someone to Dance

During the two rounds before a dance, a character may ask another character to
dance. If they are accepted, both characters are engaged to dance during the
next dancing round. If they are rejected, the asking character loses a
reputation point.

## Dancing

During the dancing round, both partners are engaged in conversation with each
other.

At the end of their collective turn, both dancers must make a roll below their
own Accomplishment score. If a dancer is accomplished in the dancing skill, they
may roll with advantage.

If both players succeed, each gains two reputation points. If both fail, each
player loses one reputation point.

# On Exploration

If not otherwise engaged, a player may explore the gala at their leisure. They
may make advantageous discoveries.

# On Combat

## Attacking

The attacking player rolls above or equal to the attacked player's stat. If the
roll succeeds, the attacked player loses a point in that stat. If a player can
indicate an aspect of their character that would benefit them in their action,
the Host may grant them advantage.

## Retort

If an attacked player wishes, they may attempt to retort by rolling an attack
roll against the attacker using the same stat. If the roll succeeds, the initial
attacker loses two points in the stat and the attacked player loses none. If the
roll fails, the attacked player loses an additional stat point.

Successful retort attacks may be retorted.

# Faux Pas

<figure class="epigraph">
<blockquote><p>It was badly done, indeed!</p></blockquote>
<figcaption>— Mr. Knightley, <cite>Emma</cite>, vol. III ch. 7</figcaption>
</figure>

If a player takes an action that is not deemed socially permissible by another
player, that other player may call Faux Pas, briefly describing the action they
considered unacceptable. They may not explain why they consider the action
unacceptable. Players may not speak until a verdict is reached. The host counts
down from three and players in agreement with the call raise their hands. If the
majority declares Faux Pas, the accused loses a reputation point. If the
majority votes against, the accuser loses a reputation point.

In the case of a tie, the Host acts as a tie breaker.

# Selling Secrets

<figure class="epigraph">
<blockquote><p>Selfishness must always be forgiven, you know, because there is no hope of a cure.</p></blockquote>
<figcaption>— Mary Crawford, <cite>Mansfield Park</cite>, ch. 7</figcaption>
</figure>

In the case that a character's reputation is running low, that character may
choose to sell any of the secrets in their hand to regain reputation. The
selling player must be engaged in conversation with the players they wish to
sell their secret to.

To sell a secret, a player must make a copy of their secret card. The reputation
point value of the copy must be one less than the point value of the original.
Upon giving this copy to another player, the seller may gain reputation points
equal to the point value on the original secret card.

A player who has been sold a secret may sell that secret in turn to other
players. They may not sell a secret to a player who already has a copy of it in
their hand.

## Crafting Rumors

<figure class="epigraph">
<blockquote><p>Seldom, very seldom, does complete truth belong to any human disclosure; seldom can it happen that something is not a little disguised, or a little mistaken.</p></blockquote>
<figcaption>— <cite>Emma</cite>, vol. III ch. 13</figcaption>
</figure>

If a player wishes, they may write down a secret that they do not have in their
hand. Upon receiving a secret card, a player may accuse it of being a rumor.

- If the secret did not originate from the accused's hand, they must let the
  accusing player know that the secret is not theirs to begin with. They do not
  need to tell the accuser who sold them the secret.
- If the secret is in fact a rumor, the accused player will lose the amount of
  reputation that they wrote on the rumor card.
- If the secret is true, the accuser will lose the amount of reputation points
  on the card.

# Outbursts

<figure class="epigraph">
<blockquote><p>I will be calm; I will be mistress of myself.</p></blockquote>
<figcaption>— Elinor Dashwood, <cite>Sense and Sensibility</cite>, ch. 48</figcaption>
</figure>

If any one of a player's stats is reduced to 0, that player may perform an
outburst. They may choose to reveal any number of secrets that they hold in
their hand to the entire party.

When a player's secret is revealed publicly, they must remove the amount of
reputation points listed on the original copy, plus the number of copies in
circulation, from their stats. All copies of the secret must be discarded from
all player hands.

If another player's stat reaches zero due to an exposed secret, that player may
perform an outburst once the initial outburst concludes.
